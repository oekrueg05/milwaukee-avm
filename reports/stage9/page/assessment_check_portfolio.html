Milwaukee Assessment Check

The city's 2025 assessment roll, compared parcel by parcel with an independent valuation model built only from public sales and property records. This version shows citywide and neighborhood-level results only; it lists no individual parcels.

Every result on this page is built from model estimates, not appraisals. Each home's estimate carries an 80% range: for about one home in five, the true market value falls outside it. A home outside its range is a place to look, not a finding that its assessment is wrong. The model cannot see interior condition, vacancy, recent damage, or legal restrictions on value.

2 · Divergence

How many homes sit far from the model

A home counts if its assessment is outside the model's 80% range and the median of five comparable recent sales (time-adjusted, not model-adjusted) points the same way. The parcel-level list is kept in a private review copy.

    3 · Groups, with a guardrail

    Do tax shifts differ by neighborhood group?

    Groups are census tracts by majority race or ethnicity and by median household income quintile (ACS 2019-2023), used only to summarize. No value is adjusted using demographics.

    Why a guardrail. The tax shift is measured against the model, so a model that under-values a group's homes makes that group look over-assessed by the same amount. Each shift is therefore corrected by the model's own measured bias for that group (cross-validation on 2021-2024 sales), with a bootstrap interval. A group is called over- or under-assessed only if (a) the corrected shift's 95% interval excludes zero, (b) the assessor's own ratio on 2025 sales, which uses no model, points the same way with an interval excluding zero, and (c) both still hold when prices are time-adjusted separately for low-, mid- and high-value homes. A result that fails only (c) is consistent with assessments lagging faster appreciation in some areas, not a valuation pattern.

    5 · Method and limits

    How the numbers were made

    Model and comparison

    • Gradient-boosted trees on the logarithm of time-adjusted sale prices, 2014-2024 sales from the city's sales files that pass this project's cleaning filters, with property features from the prior year's assessment roll, location, and nearby sale prices. The model was frozen before this comparison; it uses no assessed values.
    • Each home is valued at January 1, 2025, the roll's valuation date, with an 80% range calibrated on 2022-2024 sales (conformal quantile regression).
    • On 2025 sales, which the model never trained on, its median absolute error with the overall level removed was ; the assessor's was on the same sales.
    • Relative gap = (assessed ÷ model estimate) ÷ the citywide median of that ratio (). Implied market value = assessed ÷ the citywide level.
    • Comparable sales: same property type, searched within 0.5, 1 and 1.25 mi over the 12 months before the valuation date, then over 24 months, stopping at the first step with at least 3 sales; the home's own sales, quick-flip purchases and outlier sales are excluded. Up to 5 are kept, ranked by similarity weighted by the model's feature importance, and time-adjusted to the valuation date only (no model adjustment). Each parcel's detail shows the radius and window used.

    Tax dollars

      Excluded from the list and the tax pool

      Held-flat assessments

      Citywide, the median single-family or duplex assessment rose from 2024 to 2025 and only of homes kept exactly the same value. A value held flat often signals a special valuation (for example a condition-based or legally restricted value) that the model cannot see, so those homes are set aside rather than ranked.

      Known limits

      • Public data only. No interior inspections, condition grades, vacancy or code-violation records; no listings or appraisals.
      • Student-rental areas. Income-producing student rentals near Marquette and UW-Milwaukee carry value the model's features cannot see (rental income, leases by the bedroom). Divergence there may reflect the model rather than the assessor; this is untested, and for listed homes the comparable sales point the same way as the model.
      • Even after setting aside held-flat values and record mismatches, very low assessments outnumber very high ones among the largest gaps. Some of these likely reflect condition the public data cannot show.
      • Sale prices used in training exclude quick resales (flips) and other sales that fail the cleaning filters, which are more common in some neighborhoods; homes like those are valued from the cleaner sales around them.
      • The 2026 roll will be checked against an out-of-time test: values for Jan 1 2026, fit only on data through 2025 and committed with a hash before the city released its 2026 sales file. It is not a pre-registration; it was committed well after the valuation date.
